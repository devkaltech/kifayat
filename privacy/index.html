Privacy Policy

This policy explains what the Kifayat mobile app collects, why, where it is kept, and how to get rid of it.

Effective 5 August 2026 · Last updated 14 September 2026

The short version

  • Kifayat stores your financial records encrypted on your own device.
  • If you create an account, your records also sync to your private account on our server, over HTTPS.
  • Your transactions, amounts and notes are never sold or shared with anyone, in any form.
  • There are no ads inside the app. We do use ordinary analytics — which screens get opened, which features get used — and an advertising ID, to understand the app and to measure which adverts bring people to it. Never what you spend.
  • The app reports its own crashes, with your financial details stripped out before they are sent. What that means, exactly.
  • You can export everything or delete everything, from inside the app, at any time.
  • Kifayat never asks for your bank login and has no connection to any bank.

The rest of this page is the detail behind those seven lines. Where a line draws a distinction — between your money and your taps, between advertising inside the app and advertising for it — the distinction is the point, and the detail below says exactly where it falls.

Who we are

Kifayat ("the app") is published by Devkal ("we", "us"). For anything in this policy, write to support@devkal.com.

What Kifayat collects

1. Account information

Collected only if you choose to create an account:

  • Email address — used to sign you in and to send password-reset links.
  • Password — stored only as a cryptographic hash by our authentication provider. We never see or store your actual password.
  • Display name — whatever you type on your profile.
  • Profile photo — optional, and only the single image you pick.

2. The financial information you enter

Everything in this category is typed by you. Kifayat has no access to any bank, card or payment account, and cannot import anything on its own.

  • Transactions — amount, date, category, account, type, and your optional note
  • Accounts you create — name, type, icon, colour, opening balance
  • Budgets and their limits
  • Recurring entries such as salary, rent or subscriptions
  • Transfers between your own accounts
  • Udhaar — money you have lent or borrowed, and the name you type for the person it is with
  • Committees (kameti) — the agreement you record, what you have paid in, and when your payout falls
  • Gold — the weight and purity of what you hold

A note about the names in udhaar and committees. Those are other people, and you typed them. They are stored the same way as everything else here: encrypted on your device, synced only to your own account, never sold, never shared, and never sent to any analytics or advertising service. Please enter only as much as you need to recognise the person — a first name is usually enough, and the app never asks for anyone's phone number or address.

3. App preferences

  • Language, theme, and your notification settings
  • Whether the app lock is on

4. Crash reports

When Kifayat crashes or hits an internal error, it sends us a report so the fault can be found and fixed. This is the one thing the app sends that you did not type, and it is worth being precise about.

What a report contains:

  • Where in our own code the fault happened — file names and line numbers
  • An error label from a fixed list we wrote, such as local.write_failed
  • Your device model, operating system version, and the app version
  • A random identifier for the app installation, so we can tell "one person hit this fifty times" apart from "fifty people hit it once"

What a report cannot contain:

  • Amounts, balances, or any figure from your ledger
  • Your notes — not truncated, not partially masked, not present
  • Account or category names
  • Your email address, display name, or photo
  • Anything a database or network error quoted back at us, which is the usual way financial values leak into a crash report elsewhere

That second list is enforced in the app's code rather than promised. A crash report is assembled from a fixed set of safe fields — an error type and a code from a closed list — instead of being assembled from the error text and then filtered. Nothing is sent and later scrubbed, because scrubbing is the part that fails quietly. An automated test fails our build if any error message stops being one of the fixed codes.

The identifier is per installation, not per person. It is not your account, not your email, and not an advertising ID; it is regenerated if you reinstall, and it cannot be connected to your ledger, which never leaves your device unencrypted.

Crash reporting is handled by Firebase Crashlytics, a Google service, acting for us. Reports are not used for advertising, and Google Play requires us to have this working well enough to spot a bad release.

5. How the app is used, and the advertising ID

Kifayat sends us ordinary product analytics, through Firebase Analytics, a Google service. This is the part of the policy most apps leave vague, so here it is exactly.

What is sent:

  • Which screen you opened, which feature you used, which step of setup you reached — events with names like transaction_added and budget_created
  • Plain numbers, yes/no flags, and fixed choices from a list we wrote — for example how many accounts you have, whether the app lock is on, or which of the transaction types you picked
  • Your device model, Android version, app version, country and language
  • Your Android advertising ID, and which advert or link brought you to the Play Store listing

What can never be sent:

  • Any amount, balance or total
  • Your notes, merchant names, or the name of anyone in udhaar or a committee
  • Names you gave your own accounts or categories
  • Any free text of any kind, from anywhere in the app

That second list is a property of the code, not a promise. Analytics values in Kifayat can only be a number, a true/false, or one option from a fixed list — there is physically no way to put a piece of text into one. An automated test fails our build if that stops being true. So "we do not send your spending" is not something we remember to do; it is something the app is unable to do.

The advertising ID is a resettable number Android gives to apps. We use it to measure which adverts actually bring people to Kifayat, and to show the app to people like those who already use it. It is not linked to your ledger and cannot be used to look up anything you have entered. You can reset it, or switch it off entirely, in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads, and the app keeps working exactly as before.

Unlike Supabase and Crashlytics, which handle data purely on our instructions, Google also uses analytics and advertising-ID data for its own purposes. Under Google Play's own rules that counts as sharing, and our Data safety listing says so. We would rather write that here plainly than let you discover it on a form.

6. Where you came from

When you install Kifayat, Google Play tells the app which advert, link or campaign you arrived through. That comes from Play rather than from your phone — nothing about you is sent to get it — and we use it to work out which of our adverts are worth paying for.

What Kifayat does not collect

This list is exhaustive and deliberate. The app contains no code to do any of it:

  • No adverts inside the app. No banners, no sponsored rows, no ad network. A money app that sells screen space to a lender has picked a side, and it is not yours
  • No selling of your financial data, to anyone, in any form, aggregated or otherwise
  • No profile of what you spend, what you earn, or what you owe — the analytics described above carry none of it
  • No location data of any kind
  • No contacts, calendar, call logs or SMS
  • No bank credentials, card numbers, or payment details
  • No browsing history
  • No access to your photo library — only the one image you hand it through Android's own photo picker

The line we hold is financial data, not telemetry as a category. That is a narrower promise than "we collect nothing", and we make it deliberately, because it is one we can actually keep. Which screens people open tells us where the app confuses them. What you spend tells us nothing we have any business knowing.

Where your data is stored

On your device

Your ledger is held in a SQLCipher-encrypted database on the phone itself. The encryption key is generated on your device the first time you open the app and is kept in the Android Keystore. It never leaves the device, is never transmitted, and we do not have a copy of it.

Android's automatic cloud backup is deliberately disabled for this app, so your ledger is never copied into a Google backup.

On our servers

If you sign in, your records sync to your own account on infrastructure provided by Supabase, Inc., which supplies our database, authentication and file storage. Every request travels over HTTPS; the app refuses to connect to any address that is not HTTPS.

Access is restricted at the database level so that your rows are readable only by your own authenticated account.

Supabase operates data centres in several regions, so your data may be processed outside the country you live in. Supabase acts purely as our processor — it handles the data on our instructions and does not use it for its own purposes.

Who we share it with

Your financial records: nobody. We do not sell them, we do not share them for advertising, and we do not hand them to data brokers. Usage analytics is a different question, and it is answered honestly in the table below rather than buried.

Things worth spelling out

Fingerprint and face unlock

If you turn on the app lock, the check is performed entirely by Android. Kifayat receives only a yes-or-no answer. Your fingerprint or face data is never seen, received, or stored by this app, and never leaves your phone.

Notifications

Reminders — a budget running low, a bill due, a weekly summary — are scheduled locally on your device by the app itself. Their contents are never sent to a server, and there is no push-notification service involved.

Exports

PDF and CSV exports are generated on your device and go wherever you choose to send them. Once you share a file, what happens to it is governed by whichever app you shared it with.

Udhaar and committees — other people's names

When you record that someone owes you, you type their name. Kifayat treats that exactly like your own data: encrypted on your device, synced only to your own account, and never sent to analytics, advertising or anyone else. The other person is not contacted, is not told, and has no account here — this is your private record of an arrangement, not a shared one.

Hisaab Share

Sharing a balance builds a short piece of text on your device — a person's name and what the balance is — and hands it to whichever app you pick, or your clipboard. It is not sent to us, and there is no invitation and no shared record. Once it leaves through WhatsApp or anything else, what happens to it is governed by that app.

Gold rates

To value gold holdings the app fetches a current rate from our own server. It is a read: the request asks for today's rate and tells us nothing about what you hold. Your weights and valuations stay in your ledger.

An earlier version of this page said the app refused the advertising ID. That changed in this release, deliberately, and the table above now says so. We would rather correct it in public than leave a flattering sentence standing.

How long we keep it

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, it is removed from our live systems straight away and purged from our encrypted backups within 30 days. Data held only on your device disappears the moment you delete it or uninstall the app.

Crash reports and analytics are the exceptions, and we would rather spell that out than have you find it. Neither is attached to your account, so deleting your account does not delete them — there is no link along which to find them.

  • Crash reports age out after 90 days. What survives is a stack trace through our own code and a device model.
  • Analytics events are held by Google for a limited window set on our account, then deleted. What survives is which screens were opened on some device in some country — never an amount, a note or a name.

You can cut the advertising ID out of this at any time, without leaving the app, in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads. Resetting it makes past events unattributable to your device; deleting it stops one being sent at all.

Your rights

You can, at any time and without asking us:

  • See everything the app holds — it is all visible in the app
  • Correct anything, by editing it
  • Export the lot, as a PDF statement or a CSV file
  • Delete your account and all of its data — see below

Depending on where you live you may also have statutory rights of access, rectification, erasure, portability and objection. Write to us and we will honour them.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Data & privacy → Delete everything. This removes your account from our servers first, then wipes the local copy. It cannot be undone.

Already uninstalled the app? Use the account deletion request page.

Children

Kifayat is intended for adults and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you believe a child has created an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top of this page changes with it. Anything that materially affects how your data is handled will also be announced in the app before it takes effect.
